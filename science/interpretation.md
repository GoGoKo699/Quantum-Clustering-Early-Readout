# What may be learned before the dataset is complete?

Consider specimens arriving one at a time, each prepared in one of two unknown pure quantum states. The final task is to say which positions belong together. Names for the two groups do not matter. A natural online strategy would release preliminary conclusions and keep processing the remaining quantum data. This project determines exactly when such a classical record can be obtained while retaining the best possible eventual probability of recovering the whole partition.

The requirement is exact equality with the unrestricted Bayes optimum for the same $`n`$ specimens. It averages over independent Haar prototypes and uniform binary labels. It is a decision-performance condition, not preservation of every input state or every possible future task.

## A distinction between the last and the penultimate arrival

Suppose $`m`$ specimens have arrived. The early operation may be any quantum instrument: it can release a classical outcome, keep a quantum output, and allow the final measurement to depend on that outcome.

| Specimens still missing | Classical information compatible with the exact final optimum |
| --- | --- |
| At least two | The early record has the same probability distribution for every hidden partition. It supplies no partition information. |
| Exactly one | The record may carry a noisy statistic of the unordered sizes of the two prefix classes. It cannot identify which positions belong to either class. |
| None | The final optimal collective clustering measurement is available. |

The first row is a necessary statement about record statistics. It does not say that every uninformative operation is harmless. An operation can reveal no partition information and still damage the quantum data needed later.

The second row does not permit an exact reading of the class sizes. Even a sharp measurement of the prefix Schur sector can be too disturbing. The free information is a constrained, noisy readout of those sectors. The familiar three-qubit example lies in this final row before completion: an informative two-qubit classification can attain success $`7/12`$ without changing the optimal three-qubit score.

## One canonical readout contains all free classical information

Write $`m=n-1`$ and $`r=\lfloor m/2\rfloor`$. Let $`P_j`$ be the prefix Schur projector with second-row length $`j`$. On the support of the two-prototype ensemble, a symmetry-reduced early effect has the form

```math
M_a=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\theta_{a,j}P_j.
```

Its exact compatibility conditions are

```math
\theta_{a,j}\geq0,\qquad
\sum_a\theta_{a,j}=1,\qquad
\theta_{a,j}\leq(n-2j+2)\theta_{a,j-1}\quad(1\leq j\leq r).
```

These inequalities quantify the noise that must accompany an early count readout. They lead to a single experiment from which every free record can be generated by classical randomization. Define

```math
B_0=1,\qquad B_j=\prod_{t=1}^{j}(n-2t+2),
\qquad
q_\ell=
\begin{cases}
B_\ell^{-1}-B_{\ell+1}^{-1},&0\leq\ell\lt r,\\
B_r^{-1},&\ell=r.
\end{cases}
```

The canonical effects are

```math
F_\ell=q_\ell\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}B_jP_j,
\qquad 0\leq\ell\leq r.
```

They sum to the identity on the ensemble support. For any free reduced readout there is a stochastic matrix $`p(a\mid\ell)`$ such that

```math
M_a=\sum_{\ell=0}^{r}p(a\mid\ell)F_\ell.
```

This is maximality in the Blackwell information order: for every decision problem about the hidden partition, the canonical record is at least as useful as another exactly free record. It does not establish a universal hierarchy between their quantum output states. Different instruments realizing the same effect can leave different retained states.

An unrestricted instrument need not itself be covariant or have these physical effects. Averaging each effect over the common unitary action leaves all label-conditioned likelihoods unchanged. The theorem therefore applies to every unrestricted record through a likelihood-equivalent representative. This is why the result characterizes all available classical information without claiming uniqueness of all physical POVMs.

## A large dataset does not make exact free prefix clustering accurate

The canonical record also determines the best full-partition guess for the prefix under the free-readout constraint. For qubits, the exact examples include $`7/12`$ at $`n=3`$ and $`47/270`$ at $`n=5`$. Its large-$`n`$ behavior is

```math
P_{\mathrm{free}}(n,2)
=2^{2-n}\left[\log n+\gamma-\log4
+O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)\right],
```

where $`\gamma`$ is Euler's constant and logarithms are natural. Blindly guessing the prefix partition succeeds with probability $`2^{2-n}`$. The free readout improves this by a logarithmic factor, while its complete-prefix success still decreases exponentially.

By comparison, the inherited unrestricted final clustering optimum is asymptotic to $`8(d-1)/n^2`$ at fixed $`d`$ [Sentís et al., 2019](../references/RELATED_WORK.md). These different scores make the interpretation precise: useful quantum data can remain available for a later joint decision even when a free early classical record is severely limited.

## What the boundary does not establish

The result uses exact preservation of one finite-dataset Bayes score. A very weak informative measurement can have a very small cost. The theorem supplies no size-independent lower bound on that cost, and the final optimum itself tends to zero as the dataset grows. Approximate preservation requires a separately stated regret bound and regime.

Recovering the whole partition is also stricter than classifying most positions correctly. The conclusion is not a theorem for Hamming loss, adversarial prototypes, mixed-state clusters, noisy arrivals, or a drifting source. Nor does it show that a classical record about prototype orientation must vanish: the no-information statement concerns the partition under the specified prior.

The physical conclusion is a timing constraint on releasing classical clustering information. It is not a runtime speedup or an unrestricted quantum-memory lower bound. The [resource account](resources.md) describes an ideal realization that retains the prefix and later performs a conditional global measurement.
